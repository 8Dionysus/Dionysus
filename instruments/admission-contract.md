# Instrument admission contract

## Intended use

An admitted instrument may orient a Dionysus interview by identifying a lens,
tension, or counterexample worth exploring. It does not diagnose, certify,
rank, or define a person. Instrument results remain private source artifacts;
only reviewed, evidence-grounded claims may enter a portrait projection.

Admission applies to one exact combination of:

- instrument and form;
- language and cultural population;
- administration mode;
- scoring and interpretation method;
- purpose and participant population.

A different combination is a new candidate, not an editorial variation.

## Evidence gates

Every candidate is reviewed against all of the following gates.

1. **Construct fit** — the construct answers a named portrait question and is
   non-clinical for the intended use.
2. **Psychometric evidence** — reliability, validity, limitations, and the
   supported level of interpretation are documented in primary or authoritative
   sources.
3. **Language and culture** — the exact language form has more than a casual
   translation; adaptation and population evidence are named.
4. **Rights** — use, redistribution, adaptation, item exposure, and public-app
   permissions are explicit.
5. **Mode** — text, paper, computer, interview, and voice modes are not treated
   as equivalent without evidence.
6. **Interpretation** — norms, reference population, uncertainty, and score
   limits are stated. Percentiles are forbidden without a matching norm source.
7. **Redundancy and burden** — the instrument adds a distinct lens at a cost
   proportionate to the interview question.
8. **Privacy and review** — raw responses and scores remain private; derived
   hypotheses stay contestable and cannot bypass human review.

The translation and mode gates follow the International Test Commission's
[test adaptation](https://www.intestcom.org/files/guideline_test_adaptation_2ed.pdf)
and [computer-based testing](https://www.intestcom.org/files/guideline_computer_based_testing.pdf)
guidelines. In particular, a new delivery mode needs evidence of comparable
reliability, score distributions, external relations, item presentation, and
response control.

## Admission states

### `admitted`

The named role is allowed. `blocking_gaps` must be empty. Admission does not
expand the instrument's license or permit a stronger interpretation than its
evidence supports.

### `external-only`

The instrument may be completed through an official or otherwise authorized
surface and its private result may seed questions. Dionysus does not bundle its
items, scoring key, form, or norms. Any use must follow the external owner's
terms.

### `pilot`

One or more named gaps prevent ordinary use. During Phase 1, the entry may be
used only for research and fictional or synthetic dry runs. A real-person
pilot requires the later privacy decision as well as closure of all gaps
material to that use.

### `excluded`

The instrument or family must not route claims or present itself as part of the
portrait foundation. A person may still mention such a vocabulary in their own
story; Dionysus preserves it as self-description, not as a validated score.

## Voice postures

- `conversation-native` — an open or idiographic method designed for dialogue;
  no standardized-score equivalence is claimed.
- `unverified` — voice administration has not been shown equivalent for the
  exact form, language, and use.
- `verbatim-pilot` — wording, order, response scale, replay, and confirmation
  are fixed, but equivalence is still under evaluation.
- `equivalent` — an empirical source supports the claimed voice equivalence
  under named conditions.
- `not-applicable` — the entry is not eligible for administration.

## Interpretation law

Instrument output may produce only a scoped prompt such as:

> This result suggests a question about planning under pressure. In which
> situations does that pattern hold, where does it fail, and what concrete
> episodes support either reading?

It must not directly produce a global claim such as:

> You are a highly conscientious person.

The second statement requires episode evidence, context, alternatives,
counterevidence, and explicit human review under the portrait-claim contract.
